## Trail Highlights
We started the early-morning session from the starting point, with parakeets calling nearby. As we stepped under the canopy, we could hear faint calls of a Greater Coucal. As we headed towards Gulmohar Marg, our walk leaders pointed out that the parakeets we saw flying overhead were Rose-ringed Parakeets—the campus’s most common parakeet—though the less frequent Plum-headed Parakeet can also be found on campus.

{% include event-gallery.html section="2" size="small" %}

Throughout the walk, we caught good glimpses of campus regulars like Large-billed Crows, Rock Pigeons, and Black Kites, alongside the rolling calls of White-cheeked Barbets. We also noticed several neat cavities hollowed into tree trunks. While these closely resembled woodpecker holes, our leaders clarified they were most likely barbet nests, given that woodpeckers are rarer on campus. We also had myna chatter all around the walk. Both Common and Jungle Mynas are very common on campus, separated mainly by the facial features near the beak.

{% include event-gallery.html section="3" size="small" %}

Near the Quadrangle tree patch, we caught sight of a Greater Coucal actively foraging in the leaf litter. Our leaders shared an interesting bit of natural history: despite belonging to the same family as the Cuckoo (Koel), coucals build their own nests, unlike cuckoos. We also spotted a female (or juvenile) Asian Koel perched quietly above us.
{% include event-figure.html section="5" size="small" %}
As we approached the Main Guest House, we had the highlight of the walk: a pair of Spotted Owlets resting on a branch, giving everyone enough time to get good photographs. High in the canopy, we also caught glimpses of Red-whiskered Bulbuls. While Merlin registered the calls of an Indian White-eye and a Cinereous Tit, both managed to stay hidden in the foliage before we moved on with our walk.

P.S. There were two groups on different route, this is from perspective of the first group.
