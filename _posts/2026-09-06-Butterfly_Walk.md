## Trail Highlights

The walk started along the open road near the Faculty Club before diving into the tree canopy path. We began with a brief discussion on distinguishing features between butterflies and moths. The warm day had many butterflies out in the open, and we moved along with our butterfly experts, who shared trivia along the way as we spotted different butterfly species.

### Butterfly Facts Learned Along the way

* **Mud-puddling:** We spotted butterflies "mud-puddling"—sipping mineral-rich moisture to get essential salts.
* **Toxic butterflies and their mimics:** Chinmay explained how butterflies stay safe from predators. Blue Tigers consume toxic plants as caterpillars, making them poisonous to predators as adults. Other harmless species, like the Indian Wanderer we saw along the way, mimic the Blue Tiger's patterns to reap the same benefits (Batesian mimicry).
* **Feeding patterns:** We watched Blue Tigers rapidly flutter their wings while feeding. We learned that butterflies aren't strictly nectar feeders—many happily sip tree sap and decaying matter.
* **Lifespans & Migration:** It was also fascinating to learn about how drastically butterfly lifespans vary across species, and especially to discuss their migratory patterns.

## Notable Sightings

* **Blue Mormon:** Drifting through the sunlit tree patch.
* **Blue Tiger & Indian Wanderer:** The classic toxic original and its clever mimic.
* **Common Five-ring & Psyche:** Tiny, delicate flyers bouncing along low grasses and shade.
* **Grass Demon:** A jet-black, moth-like skipper that zips so fast it almost teleports between perches.
* **Great Eggfly & Lemon Emigrant:** Flashes of deep blue and bright yellow along the sunny trails.
