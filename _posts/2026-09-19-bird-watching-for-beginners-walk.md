The session was an introductory session for beginner birdwatchers.
The one rule for this walk was to describe but not name the bird.

**Important aspects to look for were:**
- Locate the bird: Where? On the ground, top of the tree, or lower in the tree
- Shape and relative size
- Any peculiar habit: Foraging, Movements

The aim was not to see as many birds as possible, but to observe them closely and describe them.
Since we could not name the birds, we had to note down their attributes and only at the end use the bird book to identify them using their features.

*Some from my notes: (try to guess the bird)*

- Distinct tail; longer than usual birds; and the bird moving the tail up and down slowly
- The beak was short
- A medium-sized bird, larger than the sparrows but smaller than the eagles
- Hopping on tree branches
- Brownish wings, but a darker body

Another description
- Hopping on the tree top
- Small size and streamlined body
- Greenish in color 
- Eating small fruits from a tree
- The beak was straight and not curved at the end

The birds above were a Greater Coucal and a White-cheeked Barbett

Along the way, we also learned a few basics of walking, like that it is better to move away from the sun to get good pictures, and that the best time is dawn, so there is no silhouette from the sunlight. Similarly, the best places to look in a covered area are sunlit patches where birds sometimes bask.

Some more features that help distinguish are how long birds stay in a place and whether they are constantly on the move. 
At the end of the walk, we all went through the bird encyclopedia and tried to find our birds.
Among the birds, we got a rare sight of a brown-breasted flycatcher. Other birds that we saw. We also learned how the classification of some species keeps changing over time as new findings emerge and species merge or branch out.

**Fun fact**: Coucals are part of the Cuckoo family, but unlike the most famous Cuckoos, they make their own nest.
